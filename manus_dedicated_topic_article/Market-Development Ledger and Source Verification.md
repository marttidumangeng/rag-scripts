## Analytical model assumptions

The article's TCO table is an illustrative procurement scenario, not a market-price survey or vendor quotation. It models five transport robots at a $100,000 unit assumption, a $500,000 fleet sticker price, $150,000 integration and site-preparation cost, $25,000 training and change-management cost, $60,000 annual maintenance and support, $50,000 of battery and charging reserve in year 3, $50,000 of planned software or hardware upgrade reserve in year 4, and $40,000 annual downtime/productivity reserve. The model assumes a five-year nominal horizon and excludes financing, tax, inflation, salvage value, and residual cash flows. The $18.12 per-hour BLS figure is shown as an external U.S. labour reference, but the model uses a separately labelled loaded labour assumption of $28 per hour to avoid presenting the BLS wage as a global cost.

The original analytical layer is a scope-adjusted five-year TCO bridge: the model separates vehicle capex, project delivery, workforce readiness, recurring service, risk reserve, and mid-life refresh. It then tests how the result changes when annual downtime exposure is reduced or increased. Its limitation is that it is not a statistically representative fleet benchmark and should be replaced with site-specific supplier quotes, throughput, staffing, uptime, and finance assumptions before approval.
